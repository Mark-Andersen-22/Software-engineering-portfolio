CS 255 DriverPass Project Reflection

Briefly summarize the DriverPass project. Who was the client?What type of system did they want you to design?

The client for this project was DriverPass, a company that wanted to help students prepare more effectively for their driving tests. They needed a web-based platform where customers could set up accounts, buy training packages, access online classes and practice tests, monitor their progress, and book driving lessons. The system also had to support DriverPass employees by handling customers, appointments, drivers, vehicles, reports, and updates from the DMV.

What did you do particularly well?

I believe I did a good job understanding what the client needed and turning those needs into a system design. I was able to figure out the different user types and what each person would need to do in the system. I also think my UML diagrams showed the system from several different perspectives. Making the use case, activity, sequence, and class diagrams helped me see how the requirements from the original interview could become an actual software system.

If you could choose one part of your work on these documents to revise, what would you pick? How would you improve it?

If I could change one part of my work, I would make some improvements to my Business Requirements Document. While I did identify the main functional and nonfunctional requirements, some sections could have been described more clearly and in more detail. I would go back through the client interview and make sure every requirement is explained well enough that someone else could understand exactly what DriverPass needs without needing to guess. This would also help developers use the requirements later in the development process.

How did you interpret the user's needs and implement them into your system design?Why is it so important to consider the user's needs when designing?

I interpreted the user's needs by looking through the DriverPass interview and figuring out what customers and employees would need the system to do. I used those requirements throughout my system design. For instance, the need to schedule driving lessons became part of my use case and activity diagrams, while requirements for customers, drivers, vehicles, training packages, and appointments showed up in the class diagram. I also thought about things like security, different user access levels, and making the system available online. Thinking about user needs is important because a system might work from a technical point of view but still not help the client or users if it does not actually solve their problems.

How do you approach designing software?What techniques or strategies would you use in the future to analyze and design a system?

When I design software, I start by making sure I really understand the problem and the requirements before thinking about how to build the system. I would begin by collecting information from the client and users, figuring out the functional and nonfunctional requirements, and identifying who will use the system. After that, I would use tools like UML diagrams to look at the system from different angles and break big processes into smaller, more manageable parts. In future projects, I would keep checking my designs against the original requirements as I go. This can help find missing requirements or design issues before development starts, when they are usually easier to fix.
